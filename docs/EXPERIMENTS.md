## 1. Experiment 1: Attribution vs. Counterfactual Intervention

### 1.1 Objective & Research Question
**RQ1:** *To what extent does post-hoc feature attribution (SHAP and LIME) reflect the empirical influence of evidence under counterfactual deletion intervention in a fact-checking cross-encoder?*

### 1.2 Experimental Protocol & Setup
- **Executable Script**: [`experiments/experiment1_formal.py`](file:///c:/Users/dheer/OneDrive/Documents/Fuzzy-Causal%20Evidence%20Attribution%20for%20Explainable%20RAG-Based%20Fact-Checking/experiments/experiment1_formal.py)
- **Population**: First 500 claims from `data/fever_dev.jsonl`.
- **Retrieved Context**: Top-$k$ ($k=3$) passages per claim using the hybrid retriever over the 808-passage corpus.
- **Evaluated Units**: Exactly 1,500 evidence-removal evaluations.
- **Intervention Definition**: Leave-One-Out (LOO) evidence removal:
  $$\Delta P_i = |P(\hat{y} \mid E) - P(\hat{y} \mid E \setminus \{e_i\})|$$
- **Primary Metrics**:
  1. **Top-1 Rank Agreement**: Percentage of claims where the evidence piece ranked highest by attribution matches the piece with the largest intervention influence $\Delta P$.
  2. **Per-Claim Spearman Correlation ($\rho$)**: Rank correlation across the $k=3$ evidence pieces within each claim.
  3. **Verdict Flip Frequency**: Fraction of removals that cause the cross-encoder to change its discrete classification.

### 1.3 Verified Formal Results

| Metric | Target / Description | SHAP | LIME |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Evaluated Claims** | Total claim count | 500 | 500 |
| **Evidence Removals** | Total intervention evaluations | 1,500 | 1,500 |
| **Top-1 Agreement** | Match top attribution with top $\Delta P$ | **72.20%** ($361 / 500$) | **84.00%** ($420 / 500$) |
| **Mean Per-Claim Spearman $\rho$** | Average rank correlation ($k=3$) | **0.4517** | **0.5877** |
| **Median Per-Claim Spearman $\rho$** | Median rank correlation ($k=3$) | **0.5000** | **0.5000** |
| **Verdict-Flip Rate** | Percentage of removals altering verdict | **21.47%** ($322 / 1,500$) | **21.47%** ($322 / 1,500$) |
| **Flips on Top-1 Attribution** | Verdict flips when Top-1 attributed piece removed | $268 / 322$ ($83.23\%$) | $270 / 322$ ($83.85\%$) |
| **Flips on Non-Top Attribution** | Verdict flips when Rank 2 or 3 piece removed | $54 / 322$ ($16.77\%$) | $52 / 322$ ($16.15\%$) |

### 1.4 Interpretation & Key Insights
1. **Moderate-to-Strong Alignment**: Both SHAP and LIME correlate positively with causal intervention influence, with LIME showing higher directional alignment ($84.0\%$ Top-1 agreement and mean $\rho = 0.5877$).
2. **Attribution-Intervention Discordance**: In $16.0\%$ to $27.8\%$ of claims, the top-attributed evidence piece is **not** the piece whose removal causes the largest shift in verifier confidence.
3. **Verdict Flips Without Top Attribution**: In $\approx 16.5\%$ of verdict flips ($52$ to $54$ instances), the verdict flipped upon removing an evidence piece ranked 2nd or 3rd by attribution, proving that feature attribution alone fails to reliably identify fatal counterfactual vulnerabilities.

## 2. Experiment 2: Fuzzy Evidence Reasoning vs. Linear Baseline

### 2.1 Objective & Research Question
**RQ2:** *Does fuzzy evidence-reliability scoring distinguish human-annotated gold evidence from unannotated distractor passages more effectively than a linear weighted baseline in a closed RAG setting, and what are the effects of dynamic contradiction and stability signals?*

### 2.2 Scoring Formulations
- **Fuzzy Primary Score**: `evidence_reliability` generated by the Mamdani fuzzy inference system with centroid defuzzification over $[0, 1]$. In the official frozen Phase 11 baseline, this was evaluated using the **11-rule V1 engine** (8 primary domain synergy rules + 3 baseline passthrough rules across 5 outputs). The active remediated pipeline implements the **33-rule V2 engine** providing 100% computed input-space coverage without dead zones or silent fallbacks.
- **Baseline Primary Score**: `baseline_adjusted_score` computed in two stages:
  1. Additive weighted positive score incorporating stability:
     $$S_{\text{raw}} = 0.30 \cdot r + 0.25 \cdot a + 0.25 \cdot i + 0.10 \cdot s + 0.10 \cdot s_{\text{stab}}$$
  2. Multiplicative contradiction discounting:
     $$S_{\text{adj}} = S_{\text{raw}} \cdot (1 - c_{\text{deg}})$$

### 2.3 Verified Formal Results (Dynamic Primary Condition)

#### Joint Impact of Contradiction and Stability Signals:
The secondary ablation evaluates the combined transition from schema default inputs ($c_{\text{deg}} = 0.0, s_{\text{stab}} = 1.0$) to dynamically computed signals. Under dynamic inputs, the baseline's uniform multiplicative penalty $(1 - c_{\text{deg}})$ forced **312 out of 1,500 scores ($20.80\%$) identically to 0.0**, causing a substantial loss in discrimination that dropped baseline AUROC by **$-0.1624$** (from 0.8889 to 0.7265). The fuzzy engine's non-linear rule synergies mitigated this loss, dropping by only **$-0.0316$** (from 0.7946 to 0.7630), allowing it to surpass the baseline in the presence of dynamic conflict.

### 2.6 Attribution-Gap Subgroup Findings
- **Pre-Registered Membership Subgroups ($S_{\text{gap}}^{\text{std}}, S_{\text{gap}}^{\text{ext}}$)**: Contain **$N = 0$ instances**. In this FEVER dataset, high attribution strength ($a \ge 0.60$) never occurred alongside low intervention influence ($i \le 0.40$).
- **Exploratory Rank-Divergence Subgroup ($S_{\text{gap}}^{\text{rank}}$)**:
  - $N = 87$ instances ($5.80\%$).
  - Fuzzy `explanation_confidence` median: 0.5000 (IQR 0.0000).
  - Baseline `baseline_raw_score` median: 0.3352 (IQR 0.2081).
  - Mean within-claim ranks: Fuzzy 2.23 vs. Baseline 2.30.
  - Confirmatory testing is deferred to independent corpora where attribution-intervention divergence occurs naturally.

### 2.7 Intermediate Hybrid Evaluation (`outputs/phase11_v2/`)
Results stored under `outputs/phase11_v2/` represent an **intermediate hybrid evaluation**:
- **Evaluation Setup**: The remediated 33-rule V2 fuzzy engine was evaluated using frozen Phase 10 attribution values (`SHAP_score_raw`, `LIME_score_raw`, `intervention_influence`) and historical stability signals from Phase 11, combined with conflict signals recomputed by an earlier iteration of the conflict detector.
- **Scope & Methodological Boundary**: These results confirm that the 33-rule partition successfully eliminates silent fallbacks (0.0% fallback rate vs. 59.07% in V1; 100% computed input-space defuzzification). However, they do **not** represent a full end-to-end evaluation of the latest source code (which incorporates sequence-order sensitive caching in `attribution/explainer.py` and finalized continuous harmonic mean conflict scoring in `conflict/detector.py`).
