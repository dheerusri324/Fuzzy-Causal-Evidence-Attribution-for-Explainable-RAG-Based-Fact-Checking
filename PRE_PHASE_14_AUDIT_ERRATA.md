## 1. Dataset Provenance and Development Split Lineage

### 1.1 Verified Fact
- In `data/fever_loader.py` (lines 118–168), the data ingestion pipeline connects to the HuggingFace dataset `copenlu/fever_gold_evidence` using `split="train"` in streaming mode.
- Up to the first 5,000 valid records are streamed from the training split.
- In `create_dev_subset()` (lines 171–209), exactly 250 records per label (`SUPPORTS`, `REFUTES`, `NOT_ENOUGH_INFO`) are sampled using random seed 42 (`set_seed(42)`), creating a custom 750-record balanced split.
- In line 204, each sampled record is assigned `rec.split = "dev"` and written locally to `data/fever_dev.jsonl`.
- In both formal experiments (`experiments/experiment1_formal.py` line 67 and `experiments/experiment2_formal.py` line 74), only the first 500 records of `data/fever_dev.jsonl` are loaded and evaluated (`records[:500]`).
- The 500 evaluated records comprise exactly: 168 `SUPPORTS`, 166 `REFUTES`, and 166 `NOT_ENOUGH_INFO` claims.

### 1.2 Interpretation or Limitation
- The evaluated dataset is **not the official FEVER development split** (which contains 19,998 claims released by Thorne et al., 2018). It is a custom, balanced 500-claim research subset sampled from the training split of the preprocessed `copenlu/fever_gold_evidence` repository.
- Referring to this data simply as 'the FEVER development split' without qualification misrepresents its lineage and scale.

### 1.3 Recommended Correction
- All project documentation, presentations, and paper manuscripts must explicitly state:  
  *"Evaluated on a balanced research sample of 500 claims (168 Supports, 166 Refutes, 166 NEI) sampled with seed 42 from the copenlu/fever_gold_evidence train split."*
- Discontinue any unqualified phrasing describing the evaluation pool as 'the official FEVER dev set'.

## 2. Evidence References vs. Unique Passage IDs in the Closed Candidate Corpus

### 2.1 Verified Fact
- Across the 500 evaluated claims in `data/fever_dev.jsonl`, there are exactly **540 annotated evidence references**, representing **534 distinct (unique) evidence passage IDs**.
- When partitioned by verifiable ground truth:
  - **SUPPORTS and REFUTES (334 claims):** Contain exactly **374 annotated gold evidence references**, which deduplicate to exactly **369 unique evidence passage IDs**.
  - **NOT ENOUGH INFO (166 claims):** Contain exactly **166 pseudo-evidence references**, which deduplicate to exactly **165 unique passage IDs**.
- The candidate retrieval corpus `data/evidence_corpus.json` contains exactly **808 passages**.
- This 808-passage pool was generated by deduplicating all evidence references across all 750 sampled claims in `data/fever_dev.jsonl` (534 unique passages from the 500 evaluated claims + 274 passages from the remaining 250 claims).
- Exactly **369 out of 369 (100.0%)** of the unique gold passage IDs for the evaluated verifiable claims exist within the 808-passage candidate pool.

### 2.2 Interpretation or Limitation
- The retrieval evaluation operates under a **closed, gold-conditioned candidate pool** where 100% of target evidence passages are guaranteed to be present.
- Reporting evidence counts requires distinguishing between **claim-passage references** (tokens of association; $N = 374$) and **unique text passages** (types; $N = 369$) to avoid apparent discrepancies between claim-level and instance-level audits.

### 2.3 Recommended Correction
- Whenever quoting dataset sizes, state both metrics explicitly:  
  *"The 334 verifiable claims contain 374 gold evidence references representing 369 unique Wikipedia passages, all 369 of which are present within the closed 808-passage candidate corpus."*

## 3. Evidence-Label Semantics for NOT ENOUGH INFO (NEI) Claims

### 3.1 Verified Fact
- In `experiments/experiment2_formal.py` (lines 99–100 and 469–470), gold labels are derived using:  
  `is_gold = 1 if ev.evidence_id in {ep.evidence_id for ep in rec.evidence} else 0`.
- Because `copenlu/fever_gold_evidence` attached retrieved candidate passages (from Papelo / Stammbach et al.) to NEI claims, and `data/fever_loader.py` normalized them into `rec.evidence`, exactly **147 of the 443 positive labels (33.18%)** in the official frozen Experiment 2 dataset belong to NEI claims.
- Two rigorous sensitivity protocols were established to audit this defect without modifying frozen files:
  - **Sensitivity Protocol A (All 500 Claims):** Reclassifies all 147 NEI passages as non-gold ($y = 0$), yielding 296 gold instances (19.73%) and 1,204 distractors (80.27%).
  - **Sensitivity Protocol B (334 Verifiable Claims):** Excludes NEI claims entirely, evaluating only SUPPORTS and REFUTES claims, yielding 296 gold instances (29.54%) and 706 distractors (70.46%).

### 3.2 Interpretation or Limitation
- In FEVER, an NEI claim cannot be supported or refuted by Wikipedia. Reclassifying its retrieved passages as 'not annotated gold evidence' (**$y = 0$**) does **NOT** imply that the passage is 'factually false' or 'intrinsically irrelevant'. It simply denotes that the passage does not constitute annotator-verified evidence substantiating the claim.
- Evaluating gold discrimination on NEI pseudo-evidence measured retrieval alignment with a third-party retriever rather than human ground truth.

### 3.3 Recommended Correction
- Document that the official Experiment 2 results evaluated a mixed label proxy, and present Protocol A and Protocol B as the definitive sensitivity benchmarks for pure gold-evidence discrimination.
- Strictly define negative labels ($y = 0$) as *'not annotator-verified gold evidence'* rather than *'false information'*.

## 4. Retrieval Terminology and Evaluation Bounds

### 4.1 Verified Fact
- Across the 334 verifiable claims (SUPPORTS and REFUTES):
  - Exactly **283 out of 334 claims** retrieve at least one annotated gold passage in their Top-3 retrieved candidates. The **Claim-Level Top-3 Gold Hit Rate** is:  
    $$\text{Claim-Level Hit Rate} = \frac{283}{334} = 84.73\%$$
  - Across all 374 gold claim-passage references, exactly **296 references** were retrieved in the Top-3 candidates. The **Gold Reference Recall** is:  
    $$\text{Gold Reference Recall} = \frac{296}{374} = 79.14\%$$
- In the official frozen 500-claim pool (which counted 147 NEI passages), the claim-level hit rate was reported as $430 / 500 = 86.00\%$.

### 4.2 Interpretation or Limitation
- These metrics evaluate dense bi-encoder retrieval (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) against a **closed candidate pool of 808 passages**, wherein 100% of gold passages were pre-seeded.
- Conflating this setup with open-domain Wikipedia retrieval is a severe threat to external validity. In true open-domain FEVER retrieval over 5.4 million Wikipedia articles (>30 million sentences), standard bi-encoders achieve Top-3 sentence recall of approximately 50%–65%.

### 4.3 Recommended Correction
- Never refer to 84.73% or 86.00% as 'retrieval recall' without stating the exact denominator and scope.
- Always label 84.73% as **'Claim-Level Top-3 Gold Hit Rate (283/334 verifiable claims)'** and 79.14% as **'Gold Reference Recall (296/374 references)'**, accompanied by the mandatory caveat:  
  *"Conditioned on a closed candidate pool of 808 pre-indexed passages; not open-domain Wikipedia retrieval."*

## 6. Statistical Inference Code Note: Holm-Adjusted P-Values vs. Sequential Alpha Thresholds

### 6.1 Verified Fact
- In `experiments/experiment2_formal.py` (lines 380–388):
  ```python
  step_alpha = alpha / (m - rank + 1)
  p_adj = min(1.0, p_raw * (m - rank + 1))
  p_adj = max(p_adj, prev_adj_p)  # enforce monotonicity
  rejected = (p_adj < alpha)
  ```
- In standard multiple hypothesis testing theory, there are two equivalent ways to formulate the Holm procedure:
  1. **Raw P-Value Formulation:** Compare sorted raw $p_{(k)}$ against the sequential threshold $\alpha_{(k)} = \alpha / (m - k + 1)$.
  2. **Adjusted P-Value Formulation:** Compare adjusted $p_{(k)}^{\text{adj}} = \max_{j \le k} ((m - j + 1) \cdot p_{(j)})$ against the fixed nominal $\alpha = 0.05$.
- The code correctly implements Method 2 (`rejected = (p_adj < alpha)`).
- However, in `summary.json`, the field `"step_alpha"` ($0.025$ for the smallest p-value, $0.05$ for the second) is reported alongside `"p_value_holm_adjusted"`.

### 6.2 Interpretation or Limitation
- Reporting `step_alpha` next to `p_value_holm_adjusted` can mislead a reader into mistakenly testing whether $p^{\text{adj}} < \text{step\_alpha}$, which would double-penalize the hypothesis.
- Fortunately, for all evaluated conditions (Official, Protocol A, and Protocol B), all raw and adjusted p-values ($0.0010$, $0.0020$, $0.0240$) are strictly below **both** $\alpha / 2 = 0.025$ and $\alpha = 0.05$. Therefore, **all statistical significance determinations remain 100% mathematically valid and unchanged**.

### 6.3 Recommended Correction
- In documentation tables, report either:  
  - Raw p-values tested against `step_alpha` ($\alpha / 2 = 0.025, \alpha = 0.050$), OR  
  - Holm-adjusted p-values tested against fixed nominal $\alpha = 0.05$.
- Do not display `step_alpha` as the comparison threshold for `p_value_holm_adjusted`.

## 7. Remaining Source-Level Limitations: Stability Caching and Contradiction Saturation

### 7.1 Verified Fact
Two unresolved implementation characteristics exist in the frozen experiment pipeline:
1. **Binary Collapse of Dynamic Contradiction:**
   In `conflict/detector.py` (lines 60–68):
   ```python
   conflict_detected = (max_sup > 0.5 and max_ref > 0.5)
   conflict_score = min(1.0, 2.0 * min(max_sup, max_ref)) if conflict_detected else 0.0
   ```
   Because $\min(max\_sup, max\_ref) > 0.5$ whenever conflict is detected, $2.0 \times \min(...) > 1.0$. Thus, `min(1.0, ...)` **strictly evaluates to 1.0 in 100% of conflict cases**.
   This binary step-function causes exactly $104 \times 3 = 312$ baseline adjusted scores (20.8%) to saturate at 0.0.
2. **Dynamic Signals Caching:**
   In `experiments/experiment2_formal.py` (lines 158–178), dynamic contradiction and stability signals are loaded directly from a pre-computed cache file (`outputs/phase11_formal/.signals_cache.json`) if present, rather than evaluating live NLI models during execution.
   Furthermore, in `stability/analyzer.py`, stability evaluates only a single 3-item order reversal ($k=3$), constraining Spearman correlation to only 4 discrete values $\{-1.0, -0.5, 0.5, 1.0\}$, with 90.2% of claims evaluating to $s = 1.0$.

### 7.2 Interpretation or Limitation
- These characteristics represent **unresolved source-level implementation limitations** in the frozen codebase.
- In strict adherence to audit rules, the code must not be modified, and hypothetical 'corrected' scores must not be guessed or imputed without a formal rerun.

### 7.3 Recommended Correction
- Transparently document both limitations in the Phase 14 technical report and limitations section as verified properties of the Phase 11 implementation.
- Propose unclipped continuous contradiction scaling (e.g. $2.0 \times (\min(p_s, p_r) - 0.5)$) and multi-permutation stability testing as priorities for future post-Phase-14 work.
